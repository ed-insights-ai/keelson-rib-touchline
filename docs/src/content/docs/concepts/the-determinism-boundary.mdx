A Touchline briefing is a generated document a coaching staff is expected to
trust, and one wrong number in front of a coach ends the conversation. So the
rib draws a hard line through the product: code computes every number, the
language model writes only words, and the seam between the two is a versioned
contract of named prose slots. This page walks the line: where it runs, how it
is enforced, and what it refuses to say.

## The digest owns every number

The numeric side of the line is the **digest**, assembled by `build_digest` in
`pipeline/src/digest.py` and written by the `build-digest` command to
`data/scratch/digest-{season}-{gender}-{focus}.json` under the schema tag
`touchline.digest/1`. Its docstring states the boundary in one sentence: "if a
figure is not in the digest, the report cannot say it." Season form, box-score
patterns, roster turnover, career lines, head-to-head history: every figure a
briefing may cite is computed there, from the data home's season files and
nowhere else. A language model writes prose around these figures. It never
produces one.

Two habits make a single numeric source worth trusting. First, it is honest
about what counts. `_counts` in `pipeline/src/digest.py` aggregates a fixture
only when it is final, carries both scores, and is not an exhibition, because
counting a score-less final "would invent a 0-0 draw, a clean sheet, and a
shutout at once". Coverage travels with the numbers: `match_patterns` ships
`matches_counted` beside `played_total`, so a pattern built on partial
box-score coverage arrives pre-caveated, and `minute_chart` in
`pipeline/src/report.py` prints that caveat under the goal-timing chart as "N
of M matches with a held box score".

Second, it fails rather than guesses. `build_digest` raises `ValueError` when
the home lacks the season's fixtures or the focus programme is unknown: the
caller should collect first, not guess. And the digest carries its own lineage.
Its `sources` map records each season file's `collected_at` stamp, and
`render_appendix` prints that map as the briefing's technical appendix,
rendered to its own PDF and delivered beside the report, so the collection
record travels with the claims built on it.

The digest is a regenerable intermediate, which is why it lives in the data
home's scratch space. The report workflow rebuilds it at the start of every
run, and `pipeline/scripts/build_digest.py` never touches the network:
determinism here is testable, because rerunning `build-digest` over the same
season files yields the same figures.

## The seam is a versioned contract

The model's side of the line is the **narrative**, a JSON file whose shape is
the contract `touchline.report-narrative/4`, spelled out in the module
docstring of `pipeline/src/report.py`. The contract is a fixed set of named
prose slots: a headline, a bottom line, exactly three findings each split into
evidence, meaning, and implication, at most three decisions, a landscape
paragraph, per-opponent card fields, watchlist entries. Slots carry hard word
budgets (the bottom line at most 70 words, the landscape 100, the overview
110, a watchlist "why" 90) because prose past budget breaks the print layout.

The renderer that merges the two files is plain code, and in its own words it
"decides everything that is actually a fact on the page". Tiles, tables,
charts, per-match rates, and conference medians are computed by the renderer
from digest values; narrative text is escaped and dropped into its slot. A
slot the model never filled renders as an explicit placeholder rather than
being papered over, so the deterministic skeleton always ships.

The version number in the tag is load-bearing. The narrative-writing nodes in
`workflows/touchline-report.yml` are told to read the contract before writing,
and their instructions name the digest as the only source of numbers and the
verified research pool as the only admissible news. The `validate-narrative` gate then
checks the result mechanically, starting from the exact string
`touchline.report-narrative/4` and moving through the slot counts, the word
budgets, and a grep that fails the run when machinery words such as "digest"
leak into prose. Any violation blocks the render. The gates have
[a canonical page of their own](../gates/), and the incident that made one
after every generative step non-negotiable is recorded in
[its design record](../../design/gate-after-every-generative-step/).

Because every generated word lands in a named slot of this contract, the
generative surface is enumerable: you can measure how much of a finished
briefing the model actually wrote. That measurement is a dated empirical
fact, and it lives with its date in the
[system narrative](../../design/system-narrative/), not here.

Here is the boundary on a shipped page: the tiles and the goal-timing chart
are drawn by the renderer from digest values, and the paragraph beneath them
is a narrative slot quoting those same values back.

## Words cross with labels on

Prose does not cross the seam naked. Every important claim carries an
**evidence label**, one of the four in `LABEL_NAMES` in
`pipeline/src/report.py`: `observed` (computed from published results or event
data), `projected` (a model estimate with its assumptions stated), `signal`
(rendered as "Unverified signal": an observed pattern whose cause is
unverified), and `judgment` (rendered as "Scouting judgment": interpretation
that requires film). Confidence is a separate axis: `confidence_dot` renders
high, medium, or low beside the label, so what kind of claim and how sure
never blur into one chip.

`signal` is the label that keeps the system honest about its most tempting
inference. `availability_flags` in `pipeline/src/digest.py` finds players with
large unexplained holes in their participation and says plainly that the
record cannot distinguish injury from transfer, discipline, or redshirt, so it
reports only the pattern and never the cause. The style contract in
`prompts/style-contract.md` holds the prose to the same rule: a gap is a
signal, not a diagnosis. The contract also grants the boundary's one narrow
exception: a verified news claim's own figures (a date, a poll position) come
verbatim from the cited page instead of the digest, and from nowhere else.

## What the boundary refuses to say

The strongest statement the boundary makes is a refusal. There is no
expected-goals figure anywhere in a Touchline briefing, and the reason sits
beside the renderer's contract in `pipeline/src/report.py`: shot locations are
not collected, and "a transparent absence beats false precision". The digest
scopes itself to what the box scores actually publish. `match_patterns`
aggregates shots, shots on goal, corners, fouls, saves, and goals by half
and minute band; no field in `touchline.digest/1` holds a shot location, so
nothing downstream can pretend to have one.

The same discipline decides which source feeds which number: `discipline` in
`pipeline/src/digest.py` reads season card totals from the published
statistics page rather than counting card events across held box scores,
because the statistics page covers games whose box scores were never
collected. And the style contract polices the words wrapped around the
numbers: corner counts show territorial and restart exposure, not marking
quality. Claim only what the data measures, and recommend the film audit
rather than pre-judging its result.

Observe it (read-only, under ten minutes): open a completed archived
season's `data/scratch/digest-{season}-{gender}-{focus}.json` beside its
delivered PDF and trace any three tile figures back to their digest keys;
each must be there verbatim.
