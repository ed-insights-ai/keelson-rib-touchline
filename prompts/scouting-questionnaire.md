# Preseason Scouting Questionnaire

What the report must answer, for the coaching staff of the focus programme.
Every question is answered from the digest first; the news layer adds only
verified, cited context the record cannot hold. This file is the report's
content contract — tune it here, not in code. How findings are retrieved
and verified lives in `research-protocol.md`; how the report must read and
look lives in `style-contract.md`.

**Precedence.** The questions below are the CANDIDATE set — the full sweep
the analysis performs. The style contract's page budgets, tier system, and
insight filter decide which answers survive onto the page and where they
land. An answer with no surviving home stays in the working notes; it is
never squeezed in.

**Voice.** Outside-in third person throughout: "the focus programme" or
the team's name, never "we", "our", or "us". The report is written by an
independent scouting department, and the first person leaks ownership the
document does not have.

## The executive brief (once)

- E1. **The bottom line**: in at most 70 words, what kind of season does
  the published record set up, and what must preseason settle?
- E2. **The three findings** that matter most, each split evidence /
  meaning / implication, chosen through the insight filter.
- E3. **At most three preseason decisions** — calls the staff settles
  before the first match, each passing the generic-decision test (bound to
  named players and this team's numbers).
- E4. **The preseason scorecard**: testable checks a coach can run during
  preseason matches. These render on the roster page.

## The conference landscape (once)

- L1. How did the conference actually stack up last season — record, goal
  difference, and conference-only form for every programme, in one table a
  coach reads in ten seconds?
- L2. Who returns the most production and who lost the most? Rank
  programmes by returning share of minutes and goals — and when prose
  names "the biggest single departure", it names the measure (last-season
  points or minutes) the ranking used.
- L3. Given L1 and L2 together, which programmes are rising, which are
  exposed, and where does the focus programme sit?
- L4. What changed conference-wide in the offseason that the record cannot
  see — poll movement, a wave of coaching changes, a recruiting pattern?
  (News layer: cited or absent.) L4 owns the wide view only; a single
  programme's change belongs to O10 and never repeats here.

## Per opponent (candidate set)

Cards run benchmark tier first, then swing, ordered within a tier by first
meeting date — in a double round-robin, the first of the two meetings sets
the position, and the card header carries that date.

- O1. **Form**: last season's overall and conference record, goal
  difference, clean sheets, times shut out, one-goal games. Multi-season
  trend if held.
- O2. **Goal timing**: goals for and against by half and by 15-minute
  band, with the sample stated ("18 of 18 matches held"). NCAA
  regular-season matches have ended in ties since 2022 — no overtime — so
  timing reads describe the 90 minutes. A concession pattern is a
  "potential opening" to test, never a fitness inference.
- O3. **Shot volume**: shots and shots-on-goal for and against, corners
  for and against. The record measures volume and territorial exposure —
  never write "chances" or possession, which it does not hold.
- O4. **Discipline**: yellow/red totals as a discipline profile only. Foul
  location is not tracked, so card counts never establish set-piece
  danger. No dedicated card field: it informs threat or opening prose when
  the filter passes it, and otherwise stays in the notes.
- O5. **Goalkeeping**: who kept goal, minutes, save percentage, and
  whether the keeper returns. Feeds the roster-change or potential-opening
  field when it matters; no dedicated field of its own.
- O6. **Roster turnover**: who departed and what they took, written as
  people ("the scorers of 34 of their 41 goals are gone") with the raw
  shares in tables; departed leaders named; the newcomer class sized and
  characterized (international share, verified pedigree).
- O7. **Returning threats**: the returners a coach will ask about, ranked
  by a named measure (last-season points or minutes), each with a career
  line. A trajectory claim is judgment — split the career-line evidence
  from the reading.
- O8. **Head-to-head**: the held record (since the first archived season,
  never "all-time"), current streak, and the last meeting's story.
- O9. **This season**: when and where the focus side plays them.
- O10. **What the record can't tell you** — this programme's verified
  news: a new head coach, transfers, decorated arrivals. (News layer:
  cited or absent — "no verified news" is a valid and useful answer.) O10
  owns the programme-specific item; L4 never repeats it.
- O11. **The implication**: ONE match-preparation read that follows from
  the cited numbers above, phrased as a testable preparation point, never
  a certainty. The card's implication field holds exactly one.

**Card mapping.** The card fields — identity, roster change, key threat,
potential opening, implication, up to two news items, head-to-head strip —
absorb O1 (header), O2/O3 (threat and opening evidence), O6 (roster
change), O7 (key threat), O8 (strip), O9 (header), O10 (news), O11
(implication). O4 and O5 have no dedicated home and survive only through
the filter.

## The focus programme (once, outside-in)

- F1. What the public record would show an opposing staff preparing for
  the focus side: the published stat row, goal timing, and the
  availability and usage signals anyone can read. This feeds
  executive-brief page two and the roster pages; it never re-tells the
  brief's findings.
- F2. **The summer ledger**: the verified offseason footprints of rostered
  players — summer-league minutes, national or youth-national call-ups,
  honors, notable club activity — consolidated to at most three footnoted
  signals. What counts as verified, how identity is bound, and where to
  search live in `research-protocol.md`.

## The watchlist (two per opponent)

- W1. For each rostered opponent, the two players whose held record or
  verified offseason will move first — drawn from DIFFERENT evidence
  classes (a verified offseason move, a small-sample riser, an
  availability or usage signal, a top returner). Two top scorers is a
  formula, not a pick. Each profile answers two questions: why the cited
  record says to watch them, and what preseason play would verify or kill
  the signal.
